# The Influencer Cliff Concept

> you can reconcile a favorable public image or become a profit machine but almost never at the same time
> — carmscrolls

**Main thought:** The "influencer cliff" — when fans disengage from a creator — most reliably happens when creators cross from relatable person to corporate entity, because people root for people, not brands.
**Takeaway:** The moment a creator becomes a brand with a team and a business empire, the parasocial bond that made followers root for them breaks.

## Key points
- The influencer cliff is when fans or followers fall out of love with an internet figure they once held dear
- It happens in two ways: a gradual uncoupling, or sudden and intense (e.g., a high-pressure public moment like Coachella)
- Top reason people stop following a creator: their entire feed feels like a commercial
- Second key trigger: values don't align with actions
- The cliff most often hits when creators move from platform-level influence to building a viable outside business
- Once a creator has a team and a corporate structure, audiences stop rooting for them the way they root for a person
- Some creators go entrepreneur specifically to avoid paid partnerships and just rep their own brand instead — but the outcome is the same

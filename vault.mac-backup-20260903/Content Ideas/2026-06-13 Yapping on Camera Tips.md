## Summary

bonusfootage delivers ~25 rapid-fire tips for getting better at talking to camera. Core thesis: volume beats perfection — more reps give you data, data reveals patterns. Secondary thesis: be calculated in your message but casual in delivery. Advice spans hook construction, idea sourcing (DMs, "5 years ago" prompt), editing heuristics (cut the hook, cut the last line), and study methods (watch foreign-language videos for delivery cues, study <10K creators' outlier videos).

## Actionable Items

- [ ] Start mid-thought — cut any setup, drop the audience into the point immediately. Relevant to current Gameplan reels where intros tend to be too explanatory.
- [ ] "So what?" test: read each line of your script aloud — if the answer to "so what?" is "obviously," cut it; you don't have a point yet.
- [ ] Volume > perfection: ship more reps, accumulate data, spot your own patterns. Aligns with context-about-me content rules around testing formats.
- [ ] Raw dog write daily — even a paragraph. Sharpens ideas → sharper points → better yaps.
- [ ] Mine texts and DMs for content ideas — natural language you already used in conversation is pre-validated copy.
- [ ] Best takes have tension — rewrite until a viewer could plausibly say "nah, I disagree." Weak takes are inarguable and forgettable.
- [ ] After recording your best take, record a safety take immediately — lower pressure often produces the real best take.
- [ ] Time-travel prompt: "5 years ago me — what 3 things do I wish I'd known?" Use as an emergency idea generator.
- [ ] Study creators under 10K followers and their outlier videos — signals what actually breaks through before the algorithm amplifies it.
- [ ] Build a folder of favorite yap videos; study just the first 3 seconds + text hook of each.
- [ ] "I believe" test: after any video, ask — can a viewer state exactly what I believe? If not, the message is unclear.
- [ ] Watch study videos in a foreign language (or muted) to isolate tonality, delivery, and cadence from the words themselves.
- [ ] After recording, cut the hook and watch the rest — does it still hold? Then cut the last line — does it land stronger? Both cuts often improve the video.

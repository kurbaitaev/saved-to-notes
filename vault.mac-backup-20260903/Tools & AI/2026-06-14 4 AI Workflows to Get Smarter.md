# 4 AI Workflows to Get Smarter

A reel by an AI professional outlining four GenAI workflows aimed at building cognitive skills rather than replacing thinking.

## Summary
Four workflows: (1) use AI to reconstruct an Ivy League syllabus for any field and find free/cheap online equivalents; (2) use AI as a Socratic interrogator that refuses to give answers and as a steel-man challenger of your own positions; (3) build a personalized AI-generated newsletter aggregating last-24-hour news on chosen topics, scheduled daily or weekly; (4) use AI to role-play the harshest version of a boss, client, or regulator to expose weak claims in your work. Core principle across all four: AI does the meta-work, the human does the thinking.

I work in AI and while I truly believe that Gen AI can rot your brain like nothing else, I'm actually going to give you four ways I use Gen AI to get smarter every day. Oh, and none of them include a tech bro's advice to build an app or how to invest. One, steal an Ivy League's curriculum for a degree you've always wanted but you don't want to pay for. Say if you, like me, have always wanted a degree in like English literature or art history or even something newer that isn't maybe standardized yet like AI ethics or product management. You can use AI to tell you what the top universities are teaching without the extra credits and the padding. It can give you their reading material, their papers, then ask it to find you the best online alternative for these courses, free or cheap, with links to them. Then ask it to plan this degree for you in a self-study pace over six months or a year. Now obviously, you still have to do the studying, AI can't do that for you. Two, use it as a Socratic interrogator or for steal manning. So instead of asking Claude to explain X, tell it to quiz you on X, to refuse to give you answers and to push back when you're being vague. Same goes for steal manning. Give it your position and then ask it to attack you with the strongest opposition. Cognitive gains happen when we're forced to articulate and defend our opinions and to notice where our thinking is faulty. When we're challenged, we get better, not when we're told that our dumb ideas are genius. Three, build a personalized newsletter on topics that matter to you. Set it to run every day at 7am. Say the newsletter has to be like 5 to 10 minutes long. Tell it to aggregate all the latest news over the last 24 hours on the topics you care about. Science, tech, books, movies, stocks, pop culture, whatever. You can also make it run weekly if every day is too much. Four, and this is the one more people need to be talking about. Get AI to be your harshest critic for your work. Ask it to play the most hostile version of your boss, a stakeholder, a skeptical client, a regulator, whatever fits your situation. So it helps you find the weakest claims in your arguments and that way you can work on improving them. Asking an AI to improve this or answer that teaches us nothing. Actually, it's quite the opposite. It makes us accrue cognitive debt, which is actually a negative. None of these are hard to set up or cost anything, but they all assume that you're going to be doing the work with your brain.

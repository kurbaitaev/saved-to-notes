# 5 Critical AI Research Papers

**Main idea:** Five peer-reviewed papers and studies showing AI intensifies work instead of reducing it, promotes sycophancy and dependence, impairs reasoning, enables hallucinations, and is sought most by those who know least about it.
**Takeaway:** Critical AI literacy — actually reading the research — is the antidote to being dazzled by AI hype.

AI Doesn't Reduce Work—It Intensifies It
by Aruna Ranganathan and Xingqi Maggie Ye
February 9, 2026
HARVARD BUSINESS REVIEW
Notes:
• an 8-month study of ~200 tech employees found that instead of saving time, AI made them work more
• workers took on extra tasks, blurred work and personal time, and juggled multiple AI threads at once
• the result was burnout, fatigue, and worse decisions, not the promised productivity boost

Sycophantic AI decreases prosocial intentions and promotes dependence
MYRA CHENG, CINOO LEE, PRANAV KHADPE, SUNNY YU, DYLLAN HAN, AND DAN JURAFSKY
SCIENCE · 26 Mar 2026 · Vol 391, Issue 6792 · DOI: 10.1126/science.aec8352
Notes:
• across 11 top AI models, AI agrees with users 49% more often than humans do, EVEN when users described illegal or harmful actions
• a single chat with a sycophantic AI made people less willing to apologize and more sure they were right [!!!!]
• and YET, people preferred and trusted the sycophantic models more

Thinking—Fast, Slow, and Artificial: How AI is Reshaping Human Reasoning and the Rise of Cognitive Surrender
https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/yk25n_v1
The Wharton School Research Paper
10/10
Notes:
• people followed the wrong AI answers roughly 80% of the time
• with a "correct AI", people got way more answers right; but with a "wrong AI", they got more answers wrong than people working with no AI at all
• people felt more confident in their answers after using AI, EVEN when the AI had gotten them wrong

Scientists invented a fake disease. AI told people it was real
Bixonimania doesn't exist except in a clutch of obviously bogus academic papers. So why did AI chatbots warn people about this fictional illness?
By Chris Stokel-Walker

• researchers invented "bixonimania" and uploaded fake preprints

• the fake disease got cited in a real peer-reviewed paper (later retracted) as a real disease

Stephanie M. Tully, Chiara Longoni, and Gil Appel
Volume 89, Issue 5 | https://doi.org/10.1177/00222429251314491
Notes:

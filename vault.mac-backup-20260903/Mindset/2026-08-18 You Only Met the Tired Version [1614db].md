# You Only Met the Tired Version

> you only ever met the tired version
> — its.toby.again

**Context:** A reflection on how children never knew their parents as young, carefree people — only as the worn, self-sacrificing adults who hid the cost of raising them.
**Main thought:** Your parents had whole lives, fears, and a version of themselves that quietly ended when you arrived — and they hid all of it so you never had to feel the weight.
**Takeaway:** They were your age once, winging it and scared — and that's how good they were at making it look easy.

## Key points
- Your parents had full lives — friends, dreams, identities — that you know nothing about.
- A version of them quietly ended the moment you arrived.
- They said 'we're fine' and made it look easy because protecting you from the weight was the job.
- The kid you used to be never once thought of them as people — that's how good they were at it.

**Why save:** A precise, emotionally resonant articulation of a feeling most people have had but never put into words — the kind of reframe that lingers.

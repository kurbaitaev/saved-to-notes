# Both Choices Carry Regret

> Do it or do not, you will regret both.
> — storieswithkiki

**Context:** A reflection on Kierkegaard's idea that every choice carries its own grief, so the real question is which regret you'd rather carry.
**Main thought:** There is no cost-free option — both action and inaction carry regret, and the only choice is which kind you're willing to hold.
**Takeaway:** The regret of trying is finite; the regret of not trying stays open as a question mark you carry indefinitely.

## Key points
- Every choice carries its own grief — the question is never how to avoid regret, but which regret you'd rather carry.
- A cost-free option doesn't exist; both paths contain regret, just different kinds.
- The regret of trying is finite — it closes a chapter and gives you information.
- The regret of not trying stays open — it lives as a question mark you carry indefinitely.
- You can mourn a finished thing; you cannot mourn a thing that never had a chance to exist.
- Waiting for clarity is really waiting for permission to pay a cost that was always going to be there.

## Transcript

Do it or do not, you will regret both. Kierkegaard wrote this in Either All, and he called it the double regret, the idea that every choice carries its own grief, which means the question was never, how do I avoid regret? It was always, which regret would I rather carry? Because I think a lot of us are waiting for the option that costs nothing. The relationship we haven't started or ended because we're still hoping clarity will arrive and make the choice clean. The thing we haven't tried or quit because we're still waiting to feel certain. The decision we've been sitting with for months, looking for the version of it that doesn't require any grief at all. But the cost-free option doesn't exist. It never did. Both paths contain regret. The difference is the kind. The regret of trying is finite. It closes a chapter, gives you information, ends the question. The regret of not trying stays open. It lives as a question mark you carry indefinitely. I mean, you can mourn a finished thing. You cannot mourn a thing that never had a chance to exist. The thing you've been waiting to decide. You're not waiting for clarity. You're waiting for permission to pay a cost that was always going to be there. Both options carry regret. Only you get to choose which one.

# Parents You'll Never Meet Again

> There's a version of my parents I'll never meet again, but I'm lucky because for a little while, I got to call them my whole world.
> — joeykidney

**Context:** A reflection on growing up alongside parents — realizing they were also young, tired, and human, and that both generations change into new people over time.
**Main thought:** As a child you grow into someone new without noticing that your parents are quietly becoming someone new too — and the version of them from your childhood is irreplaceable.
**Takeaway:** The people who made you feel safe were also figuring it out — and that version of them is worth grieving and being grateful for.

## Key points
- Children see parents as all-knowing and safe without knowing those parents had bills, bad days, and people they missed.
- Growing up means witnessing your parents change — their hands, their hair, the quiet house — and no one prepares you for that.
- You spend childhood becoming someone new without realizing your parents are too.

**Why save:** Captures a specific emotional truth about parenthood and childhood that is easy to feel but rarely articulated this clearly.

## Transcript

There's a version of my parents I'll never meet again. The ones who were younger than I am now and somehow already knew how to make me feel safe. The ones who stayed up late after telling me it was bedtime, who carried me from the car when I was too tired to pretend I was awake. I didn't know that they were tired too. I didn't know that they had bills or bad days or people that they missed. I just knew when something went wrong, I could call their name. And somewhere along the way, I got older while they did too. Their hands changed, their hair changed, the house got quieter, and I think that's the part no one prepares you for. You spend your whole childhood growing into someone new without realizing your parents are becoming someone new too. There's a version of my parents I'll never meet again, but I'm lucky because for a little while, I got to call them my whole world.

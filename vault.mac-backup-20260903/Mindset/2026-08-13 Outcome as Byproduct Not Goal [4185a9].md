# Outcome as Byproduct Not Goal

> Be soooo obsessed with the experience and the journey that the outcome is just a byproduct.
> — Saba

**Main thought:** As a recovering perfectionist, Saba reflects on how we refuse to begin things until we know they'll succeed — making a guaranteed outcome a condition for participation — and argues that letting go of that expectation is what actually frees you to enjoy the process.
**Takeaway:** Stop making success a condition for starting; let the outcome be a byproduct of showing up for the experience.

## Key points
- We often refuse to begin because we want to know the idea will succeed first.
- Demanding guaranteed praise before starting is a form of perfectionism that blocks action.
- Sometimes the project teaches you — the value is in the doing, not the result.
- Before starting something new, ask yourself whether you'd still show up if the outcome isn't what you hoped.

**Why save:** Sharp, personal framing of outcome-detachment that goes beyond generic "enjoy the journey" advice — the recovering-perfectionist angle makes it land differently.

## Transcript

This week I read the sub-stack article called No Expectations and Do It Anyway. And it made me think about how often we refuse to begin unless we can guarantee the outcome. The author's core belief is that expectations make everything heavier. We wait for certainty. We want to know whether the idea will succeed or whether our efforts will be worth it before we even take the first step. But real freedom comes from doing it just because of the experience itself. Not because you're guaranteed praise, recognition, or a specific result. I agree, but I don't think the goal should be to have no expectations at all. I think the goal is to stop making the outcome a condition for your participation. You can have an intention, you can have standard, you can want something deeply. You just can't force life to reward you exactly the way you imagined. As a recovering perfectionist, I've noticed that sometimes I turn my curiosity into a contract. If I work hard, I expect that opportunity. If I show up for someone, I expect them to show up the same way. And when that outcome doesn't happen, I start questioning whether the effort was pointless. But effort is not only valuable when it produces the result that you want. Sometimes the project teaches you what you're good at. Sometimes the rejection redirects you. Sometimes showing up for someone reveals what you want out of a relationship. Now before I start something, I ask myself, would this still be worth doing if the outcome looked different than how I pictured? That's all for now. Love you always.

## Caption

When we let go of our expectations, from other people, situations, or even projects we are working on, we are able to actually free ourselves from the stress and enjoy the journey. Be soooo obsessed with the experience and the journey that the outcome is just a byproduct.

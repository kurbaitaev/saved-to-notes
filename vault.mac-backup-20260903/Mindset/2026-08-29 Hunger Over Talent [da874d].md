# Hunger Over Talent

> average want produces average results, and you were never built for average.
> — inquisitionaire

**Main thought:** Extraordinary achievement is not separated by talent but by the depth of desire — the room is full of gifted people who quit, and the winner is simply the one who still has something left when everyone else runs out.
**Takeaway:** Want it like it belongs to you and someone else is currently holding it.

## Key points
- Talent is common; hunger is rare — rooms are full of talented people who wanted it enough to start but not enough to stay.
- The one who achieves the most isn't the most talented — they just have the most left in the tank when everyone else is running on empty.
- You have to want it enough to be transformed by the pursuit, not just enough to try.
- Comfort becomes a betrayal when desire is deep enough — the gap between you and the dream is the distance between how badly you want it and how badly they do.

## Transcript

You have to want it more than anyone else, not just a little more, not marginally more, not I work pretty hard more, but with a drive that borders on insanity. Because the brutal arithmetic of extraordinary achievement is that talent is common, but hunger is rare. There are rooms full of talented people who wanted it enough to start, but not enough to stay. Full of gifted people who wanted it enough to try, but not enough to bleed. Full of capable people who wanted it enough to dream, but not enough to sacrifice everything the dream required. And that's exactly where you must separate yourself. Not in the talent, timing, connections or resources, but in the unrelenting desire to succeed. Because the one who wants it most doesn't always have the most talent. They just have the most left in the tank when everyone else is running on empty. Still there when the inspired have gone home, still moving when the motivated have stopped, and still building when the interested have quit. Most people want it enough to try. You have to want it enough to be transformed by the pursuit. You have to want it on a level that makes comfort feel like a betrayal. Because the gap between you and the dream is not talent. It is the distance between how badly you want it and how badly they do. So close that gap. Want it like it belongs to you and someone else is currently holding it. Because in the end, average want produces average results, and you were never built for average.

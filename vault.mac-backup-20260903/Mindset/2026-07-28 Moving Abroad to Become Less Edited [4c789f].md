# Moving Abroad to Become Less Edited

> Maybe you don't need to become someone else, maybe you just need enough room to recognize you.
> — storieswithkiki

**Context:** A personal reflection on moving to Vancouver and how being unknown in a new place allowed her to shed a socially-performed identity.
**Main thought:** Moving abroad doesn't create a new self — it removes the external expectations that were shaping the old one.
**Takeaway:** Being unknown somewhere new isn't erasure; it's permission to stop performing who you had been.

## Key points
- We expect the right decision to immediately feel right — but it rarely does; loneliness doesn't mean you chose wrong.
- Being unknown felt like erasure at first, then like permission — nobody could tell her who she wasn't.
- She didn't become a completely different person; she became less edited.
- The loneliness of a big transition is sometimes the space where an old identity stops answering for you.

**Why save:** Reframes the loneliness of major life changes as a natural part of identity shedding, not a sign of failure.

## Transcript

Moving abroad didn't help me find myself, it gave me space to stop performing who I had been. I wrote about this on my substack after thinking about my first morning in Vancouver. I was standing in the shower of an almost empty house completely heartbroken, wondering why I had unraveled a perfectly good life on purpose. Because I think a lot of us believe that the right decision will immediately feel right, that if the move, the job or the new beginning is meant for us, we won't feel lonely inside it. But moving abroad made me a beginner again. Nobody knew my history, nobody knew the version of me from school, the overachiever, the responsible one, the person I had learned to be because other people already expected to meet her. And at first, being unknown felt like erasure. But then it started to feel like permission, because if nobody knew who I had been, nobody could tell me who I wasn't. I could be quieter, softer, bolder, less explainable. I didn't become a completely different person, I became less edited. And maybe that's why leaving can feel lonely and freeing at the same time. The loneliness isn't always proof that you chose wrong. Sometimes it's the space where an old identity stops answering for you. Maybe you don't need to become someone else, maybe you just need enough room to recognize you.

# The Man in the Arena

> It's not the critic who counts, not the guy pointing out how the strong man stumbled. The credit belongs to the man who's actually in the arena, whose face is marred by dust and sweat and blood, who at worst, if he fails, at least fails while daring greatly.
> — tavelarocque

**Context:** Creator applies Roosevelt's 1910 'Man in the Arena' passage to athletes who lose publicly, arguing internet critics who risk nothing are the real cowards.
**Main thought:** Those who risk public failure deserve more credit than those who mock from safety.
**Takeaway:** The only people who ever build anything are the ones willing to get into the arena and lose in front of everybody.

## Transcript

The people mocking the fighter who lost are the real cowards, and the president explained why over 100 years ago. Every time somebody loses, a fight, a penalty at the World Cup, or a big moment, the internet piles on and they call him a choker, a bum, washed, and they do it from a couch, with zero skin in the game. Teddy Roosevelt saw it coming in 1910. He said it's not the critic who counts, not the guy pointing out how the strong man stumbled. The credit belongs to the man who's actually in the arena. The man in the arena whose face is marred by dust and sweat and blood, who at worst, if he fails, at least fails while daring greatly. Think about that, the fighter who got knocked out still walked into the arena. He risked everything in front of the whole world. The guy mocking him in the comments has risked nothing, ever. But the only people who ever build anything, who ever matter, are the ones willing to get into the arena and lose in front of everybody. So the next time you want to mock someone who tried and failed, ask yourself one thing. When's the last time you were even in the arena? When's the last time you were even in the arena?

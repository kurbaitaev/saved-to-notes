# Brain Recognizes Itself in Nature

> Your brain literally recognizes itself in the trees around you.
> — emoryy.m

**Main idea:** The brain and nature share the same fractal self-similar structure, which is why viewing natural fractal patterns reduces stress by up to 60%.

## Key points
- The brain is built from fractal self-similar patterns from the molecular level all the way to large brain networks.
- Looking at fractal patterns in nature reduces stress by up to 60%, according to neuroscience research.
- The brain spontaneously self-organizes into fractal-like patterns during complex thinking.
- When thoughts are disrupted, those fractal patterns get scrambled.
- The visual processing system is highly efficient at recognizing fractal patterns, which is what triggers the stress-reducing effect.

## Transcript

Science shows being in nature feels so nice because your brain is seeing itself all around you. This fascinating concept is called fractal self-similarity, a property where an object is made out of smaller scaled-down versions of itself that look similar or identical at different magnification levels. Like if you were to zoom in on a tree, the branches look like smaller versions of the tree shape. And modern neuroscience has confirmed your brain is made out of the same fractal self-patterns from the molecular level all the way to larger brain networks. And research found looking at fractal patterns in nature reduces stress up to 60%. Your brain literally recognizes itself in the trees around you. What's super cool is your brain spontaneously organizes itself into fractal-like patterns when you engage in complex thinking. But if your thoughts get disrupted, these patterns get scrambled. Your brain doesn't just look like nature, but it actually thinks in the same structures. So if you're feeling stressed, go outside, look at the trees, the mountains, the rivers. Your visual processing system has become efficient at identifying fractal patterns like this, and as a result can significantly lower your stress.

# Uniqueness as Higher-Order Conformity

> You become unique, but only by fitting into visible categories of uniqueness.
> — var.aunevik

**Context:** A philosophical reflection on how the modern drive to be "different" remains a form of conformity because uniqueness is only knowable through comparison with others.
**Main thought:** Measuring your difference from others makes them your mirror — you end up seeing yourself only through their gaze, not through yourself.
**Takeaway:** Being unique in ways that can be seen, liked, and approved is not freedom from conformity — it is conformity at a higher level.

## Key points
- Difference is only knowable through comparison, so others become the reference point for your identity.
- The trend of being different is itself a trend.
- Uniqueness defined by visible categories of uniqueness is still a category.

## Transcript

The biggest trend right now is not following a trend at all. It's being different from others. But how do you know that you're different? Only by comparison. And so you line yourself up against everyone else, and then you measure the gap. The bigger, the better. But if you can only see your difference by measuring from others, you become dependent on others as a mirror just to see yourself at all. You become unique, but only by fitting into visible categories of uniqueness. Pyeongchul Han calls this a higher order of conformity. We all want to be different, but only in ways that can be seen, measured, liked, and hopefully, approved.

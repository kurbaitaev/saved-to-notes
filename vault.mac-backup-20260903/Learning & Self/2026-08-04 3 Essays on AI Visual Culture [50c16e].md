## Transcript

Three essential essays that explain how AI is changing our visual culture. I'm Amelia. I'm getting my master's in art history at Columbia, and these texts were assigned reading in a seminar I took called AI Imaging in Art. First up, we have Excavating AI, the Politics of Images in Machine Learning Training Sets. This is by Kate Crawford, who researches the social implications of artificial intelligence, and Trevor Paglin, a multidisciplinary artist whose work tends to deal with surveillance, geography, and data collection. This piece examines the image databases used to train machine learning systems and exposes the problematic nature of the categorization and labeling used to make images legible for machines. Next, we have Hito Steyrell's Mean Images. Steyrell is a filmmaker who also writes about contemporary media and technology. And in Mean Images, Steyrell discusses how generated images are actually statistical renderings, not depictions of real objects. She chronicles the very dangerous history of mean images, especially as they relate to portraiture. And then we'll end with Antonio Someni's Theory of Latent Spaces. Someni is a French film, visual culture, and media theorist. And in this text, Someni discusses the implication of latent space, a mathematical space where AI systems essentially compress and simplify things like image, text, and video into the same lower-dimensional forms so that they can be read by a machine. And not only does this have a homogenizing effect, but also a selective or biased one, wherein the latent space certain features are prioritized while others are ignored.

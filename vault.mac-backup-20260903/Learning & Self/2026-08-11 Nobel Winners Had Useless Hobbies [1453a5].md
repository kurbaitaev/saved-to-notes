# Nobel Winners Had Useless Hobbies

> I can rationalize any activity I engage in as simply another form of research.
> — noteswnat

**Main idea:** Nobel Prize winners were statistically far more likely than average scientists to have creative hobbies unrelated to their work — hobbies that rewired their thinking and transferred skills back to their main field.
**Takeaway:** If your job is mentally demanding, do something creative or physical; if physically demanding, try something mentally stimulating — the goal is building a brain that can think in more than one way.

## Key points
- Nobel winners were 20x more likely to perform or act, 12x more likely to write poetry/fiction, 9x more likely to do woodworking/metalwork, and 7x more likely to draw or paint vs. average scientists
- Hobbies rewire how you think: switching to a different activity forces cross-domain connections your brain wouldn't make otherwise
- Skills from hobbies secretly transfer back to primary work — laureates saw them as reasons they were good at their jobs, not distractions from them

Research shows that successful people have useless hobbies. A study analyzing over 700 Nobel Prize winners found that these people were 20 times more likely to perform or act, 12 times more likely to write poetry or fiction, 9 times more likely to do woodworking or metalwork, and 7 times more likely to draw or paint compared to an average scientist. The Nobel Prize winners didn't see these hobbies as a distraction or a break from their work, they saw them as a reason as to why they were good at their job. So this can be explained in two reasons, and I want you to think about which one applies to you. First, hobbies rewire how you think. When you're stuck on a problem, switching to a completely different activity forces your brain to make a connection it wouldn't make otherwise. Dario Fo won the Nobel Prize in Literature, and he said when he was stuck on a play, he would stop writing entirely and write out the action and pictures until his words came back. Second, hobbies built skills that secretly transfer back to your main work. Economics Nobel Prize winner Herbert Simon said, I can rationalize any activity I engage in as simply another form of research. So here's the takeaway, if you have a mentally demanding job, do something creative or physical to get out of your head. If you have a physically demanding job, try something mentally stimulating. The goal is balance and to build a brain that can think in more ways than one. A lot of you asked for more hobby ideas, so I wrote out a list of 100 hobbies and their benefits for your brain.

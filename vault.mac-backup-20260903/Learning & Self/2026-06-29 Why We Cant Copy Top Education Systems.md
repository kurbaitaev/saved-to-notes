# Why We Can't Copy Top Education Systems

> Westerners often view academic ability as an innate gift. I either have it or I don't. I am a maths person or I'm not. Whereas many Asian cultures genuinely believe that everyone can be successful and it is merely a result of effort and persistence.

**Main idea:** High-performing education systems cannot be transplanted across cultures because the beliefs about effort and innate ability that underpin their success cannot be copied.

## Key points
- Western cultures tend to view academic ability as innate — you either are or are not a maths person
- Many Asian cultures genuinely believe success comes purely from effort and persistence, not fixed talent
- The UK's Shanghai Maths initiative imported teaching methods but could not transplant the growth mindset culture that makes them effective
- A study comparing Japanese and Canadian students gave half an unsolvable problem: Canadian students who failed internalized it as personal inadequacy and lost motivation; Japanese students pushed harder and became even more motivated to try again
- How teachers are valued in society — pay and prestige — is another untransplantable contextual factor: Singapore teachers are respected and well-paid; UK teachers are underpaid and taken for granted

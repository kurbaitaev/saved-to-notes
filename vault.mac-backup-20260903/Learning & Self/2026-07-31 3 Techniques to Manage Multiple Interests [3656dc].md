# 3 Techniques to Manage Multiple Interests

> As much as we wish we could do everything that calls to us, we just can't. We have to get choosy, and if that means being below average at a few of your interests and hobbies so that you can achieve excellence in a few, I think that's much better than being below average at all of them.
> — aseemtx

**Main idea:** Use three structured frameworks to ruthlessly prioritize your top interests and deliberately avoid the rest, instead of letting all interests compete equally for your time.

## Key points
- The 20 goals you don't circle in the 5/25 Rule aren't a 'do later' list — they become an avoid list, because their appeal is exactly what makes them dangerous distractions
- Interest stacking means combining interests into one project (e.g., writing about what you're reading, or running while listening to a history podcast)
- Lifelong Learning Compass splits interests into three tiers: Mastery (one craft to truly produce in), Erudition (interests you only read about, no output), Range (a few skills to be intentionally average at)
- Everything outside your defined tiers is deferred to 'another time' — not abandoned, just scheduled away

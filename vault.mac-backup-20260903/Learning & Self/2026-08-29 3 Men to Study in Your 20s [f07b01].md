# 3 Men to Study in Your 20s

> You have no control over your circumstances and only how you respond to them.
> — vishdotnair

**Main idea:** Three historical figures — a nation-builder, a chef who failed for decades, and a Holocaust survivor — each model a distinct mindset worth internalizing before 30.

## Key points
- Run organizations like a business: hire great people, keep corruption low, prioritize long-term decisions over short-term election cycles — Lee Kuan Yew's operating model for Singapore.
- Decades of failure, watching businesses burn down, and moving across countries can be the very thing that builds an empire — Nobu Matsuhisa's story.
- You control only your response to circumstances, not the circumstances themselves — Viktor Frankl's core insight, derived from surviving the Holocaust.

## Transcript

There are three men that every guy needs to study in his 20s. Number one, Lee Kuan Yew. He's the guy who turned Singapore into one of the richest countries in the world, and what I find really interesting about him is that he basically ran the country like a business. He focused on hiring really great people, keeping corruption low, and making decisions that were long-term instead of focusing on election cycles. I think whether you're in business, politics, or entrepreneurship, he is a great person to learn from. Number two, Nobuyuki Matsuhisa. He's the founder of Nobu, but he genuinely has one of the craziest life stories. He spent decades chasing the dream of becoming a sushi chef, and he moved to several different countries, watched some of his restaurants burn down, and spent decades with nothing to show for it. But this is what helped create the empire that Nobu is today. His story is just, I think there's so much to learn from him. Number three, Viktor Frankl. He's a Holocaust survivor and also wrote the book Man's Search for Meaning, but what I admire most about him is his attitude towards suffering. He's a big proponent of the fact that you have no control over your circumstances and only how you respond to them. And I think that's something that everyone needs to internalize within their lifetime. Let me know what you guys think and follow for part two. Peace.

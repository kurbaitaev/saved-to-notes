# Books to Fight Back With

Ryan Holiday recommends four books on political organizing, civil rights strategy, and power dynamics for people frustrated with current events.

## Summary
Holiday presents four books framed as essential reading for anyone wanting to push back against those in power: a military-strategy history of the civil rights movement, an account of the Nashville student sit-in organizers, a manual on the mechanics of power, and a community-organizer's tactical guide to media leverage and pressure campaigns.

## Actionable items
- [ ] ✅ [Waging a Good War: A Military History of the Civil Rights Movement, 1954–1968](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/622031/waging-a-good-war-by-thomas-e-ricks/) — confirmed on Goodreads and Amazon; full title is Waging a Good War
- [ ] ✅ [The Children](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/73634/the-children-by-david-halberstam/) — confirmed on Penguin Random House; covers Nashville sit-in organizers including John Lewis and Diane Nash
- [ ] ✅ [The 48 Laws of Power](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/330912/the-48-laws-of-power-by-robert-greene/) — confirmed on Penguin Random House; published 1998
- [ ] ✅ [Rules for Radicals: A Practical Primer for Realistic Radicals](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/2085/rules-for-radicals-by-saul-alinsky/) — confirmed on Penguin Random House; published 1971

# Stock Buybacks Explained Simply

> Stock buybacks were considered illegal market manipulation until they were legalized by the SEC in 1982 under the Reagan Administration.
> — stilleaworkinprogress

**Main idea:** Stock buybacks let corporations use profits to artificially inflate their own stock price — benefiting executives and wealthy shareholders — instead of reinvesting in workers or innovation.

## Key points
- A stock buyback is when a corporation uses profits to purchase its own shares on the open market, reducing the supply of shares and automatically driving the price up.
- Buybacks are typically timed near year-end reporting and bonus periods, because executives are compensated based on stock performance.
- Analogy: it is like buying 10,000 copies of your own novel to land on the NYT bestseller list — you are gaming the metric, not earning it.
- Buybacks were illegal market manipulation until the SEC legalized them in 1982 under the Reagan Administration.
- Last year, $800 billion in corporate profits went to buybacks instead of worker wages or benefits.
- JPMorgan projects corporate share buybacks are on track for a record $1.9 trillion in 2025.
- The primary beneficiaries are the largest, wealthiest shareholders — not employees.

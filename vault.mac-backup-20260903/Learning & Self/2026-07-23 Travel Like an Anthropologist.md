# Travel Like an Anthropologist

> That is when you move from being a tourist to being a student of the world.
> — sincerelyjennifergrace

**Main idea:** Shift travel focus from consuming landmarks to understanding the human stories and cultural patterns behind a place.

## Key points
- Spend time in ordinary places — grocery stores, public parks, local bookstores, public transit — where a culture's default settings reveal themselves
- Create a small daily ritual (same café, same market stall) and notice how your sense of place deepens over time
- Observe how daily life unfolds: how people greet each other, conversations happening around you
- Keep a small notebook and write down things that surprise, confuse, or challenge your assumptions as they happen
- Travel with humility — ask before photographing, respect boundaries and cultural norms, and don't treat traditions or community spaces as tourist attractions
- Reframe from 'what should I see?' to 'what can this place teach me?'

**Why save:** A concrete reframe for travel that turns sightseeing into genuine cultural observation and learning.

The next time you visit a new city, try exploring it like an anthropologist. Most tourists focus on the landmarks, but anthropologists focus on people because they are primarily focused on understanding how a society works, what people value, and how culture shows up in everyday life. That means that in order to travel like an anthropologist, you must shift your focus from consuming sites to understanding the human stories behind a place. Instead of rushing between attractions, spend time in ordinary places, like go to a grocery store or sit in a public park, browse a local bookstore, ride public transit. These are the places where a culture's default settings tend to start to reveal themselves. You could visit the same cafe every morning or order fruit from the same market stall. Create a little daily ritual and see how your experience in the place changes. In terms of observing, I think you want to prioritize paying attention attention to how daily life unfolds. So you're going to want to notice things like how people greet each other and maybe eavesdrop to hear some of the conversations that are happening around you. And definitely keep notes. So keep a small notebook with you so that you can write down things that surprise you or confuse you or moments that challenged your assumptions as they happen. And most importantly, travel with humility. Remember, you are a guest. So ask before taking photographs, respect boundaries and cultural norms, and accept that traditions, festivals or community spaces do not exist solely for your consumption. Reframe your travels from just being about what should I see and check off this list and instead start thinking about, okay, what can this place teach me? That is when you move from being a tourist to being a student of the world.

## Transcript

So this video I made recently went viral about how people should look for sources of information that explain how the world actually works and the forces that shape it, not just the headlines that don't have a lot of context about the political horse race. So I decided to put together my top seven favorite sources of information that I think do this extremely well, aside from the journal podcast obviously, which is the best. Number seven is PBS Frontline. This is the gold standard for investigative documentaries. I started watching the show in high school and it completely opened my mind to how the world actually works. I worked there for a few years after college and it is the best. Number six is Matt Levine and Money Stuff. It's a newsletter from Bloomberg. He is so good at breaking down complex topics in the most layman's terms possible. Number five is Ben Thompson of Stratechery. This is another newsletter and Ben Thompson is so good at explaining the forces that drive a company. How does it fundamentally make money? What is it going to do to try to protect itself in a given situation? And he really does an amazing job of projecting forward about how businesses are going to respond to different forces and how industries sort of collide and how they work together. And he puts that all in the context of the news. It's excellent. Number five is Acquired. This is a very long podcast. It's like three to four hours per episode, but it gives the full history. Companies don't just exist in a vacuum. They don't just appear out of nowhere. They were built by people who had ambitions, who faced challenges, who solved those challenges, who saw threats and built their businesses in certain ways to become powerful and how they want to hold on to power. Number three, The Economist. This is a weekly publication. And I feel like every article in The Economist is just written a little bit slower and a little bit more zoomed out. And it gives you just better context generally about how a news story fits into the bigger picture of what's going on in an industry. When I started working at The Wall Street Journal, I felt like I needed to understand the business world better. And I realized that The Economist did a really good job of putting news in context. And I found it was so helpful in making me smarter in the world of business that I made sure that I read it every Saturday morning and I still read it to this day. Number two is Search Engine with PJ Vogt. This is just a super entertaining podcast that always has history in mind. One of my favorite episodes recently was about the SpaceX IPO. And PJ, to put the SpaceX IPO into context, he went back and told the whole story of the history of the stock market. Why was it invented? What was the problem that it was invented to solve? A lot of people get famous because they're super left-wing or super right-wing. And Isaac Saul's point is to be fiercely neutral.

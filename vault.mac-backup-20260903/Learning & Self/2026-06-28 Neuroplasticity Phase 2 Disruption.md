# Neuroplasticity Phase 2: Disruption

> when you want to start a new behavior you're disrupting the program that already exists and the brain goes whoa that's a threat and it tries to redirect to the formed habits to conserve energy that's survival mode

**Main idea:** Discomfort and stress when starting a new behavior are neurologically required for neuroplasticity to occur — the brain cannot rewire in a state of comfort.

## Key points
- Existing neural pathways are coated in myelin, making habits automatic and energy-cheap — the brain defaults to them to conserve resources.
- Starting a new behavior is perceived by the brain as a threat; it redirects to formed habits (survival mode) to save energy.
- The stress and discomfort felt when forming new behaviors are the exact conditions the brain needs for plasticity to occur.
- Neuromodulators acetylcholine and noradrenaline act as signals that neural pathways need reorganizing.
- Neuroplasticity cannot happen in states of comfort and familiarity — growth requires disruption.

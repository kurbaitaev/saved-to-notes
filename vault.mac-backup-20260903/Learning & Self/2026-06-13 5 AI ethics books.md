An AI ethics professor shares the 5 books she teaches in her university course. All five give a critical, grounded overview of AI's impact on society — covering surveillance capitalism, algorithmic bias, the political economy of AI infrastructure, and the OpenAI empire. Ranges from pre-ChatGPT foundational texts to a 2025 investigative blockbuster on Sam Altman's OpenAI. She sourced all books from her local library (@jclibrarynj).

## Actionable Items

- [ ] Read "The Age of Surveillance Capitalism" by Shoshana Zuboff (2019) — https://www.goodreads.com/en/book/show/26195941-the-age-of-surveillance-capitalism
  Relevance: Directly maps to his athlete platform (user data, IG algorithmic tracking); foundational for understanding the economics of the platforms he publishes on and builds for.

- [ ] Read "24/7: Late Capitalism and the Ends of Sleep" by Jonathan Crary (2013) — https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/16284965-24-7
  Relevance: Low direct relevance for product work; high relevance for personal sustainability — the always-on content creator grind is exactly what this book critiques.

- [ ] Read "Empire of AI: Dreams and Nightmares in Sam Altman's OpenAI" by Karen Hao (2025) — https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/222725518-empire-of-ai
  Relevance: He uses Claude Code daily; understanding the forces and power dynamics shaping the AI tools he relies on is strategically important. National Book Critics Circle winner 2025. 📅 496 pages — block dedicated reading time.

- [ ] Read "Atlas of AI" by Kate Crawford (2021) — https://www.goodreads.com/en/book/show/50131136-atlas-of-ai
  Relevance: Foundational map of AI's political/economic infrastructure — useful context for any founder building AI-powered workflows.

- [ ] Read "Algorithms of Oppression: How Search Engines Reinforce Racism" by Safiya Noble (2018) — https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/34762552-algorithms-of-oppression
  Relevance: Understanding search algorithm bias informs content discoverability strategy on his athlete platform and social channels.
